I was delivering food and worked as a courier on my electric bike for 4 years, mainly doing Uber Eats and Stuart (the delivery network for Just Eat), and Deliveroo. Where I lived and rode in Sheffield, the hills are punishing, and using an electric bike is the only sensible way to put in consistent 6 to 8-hour shifts without burning out your legs or ruining your knees.

Using an e-bike is far more accessible than a car or motorbike: you don’t need proof of commercial courier insurance, road tax, or an MOT. Running costs are literally a few pence per overnight charge, maintenance is a fraction of a scooter's, and you can weave through peak-hour city gridlock while car drivers sit stuck at traffic lights.

I have a lot of experience in this job — and at times I had to push a 30kg dead bike home up hills because I misjudged my battery. Many delivery riders start out underprepared and buy the wrong bike. This guide will save you from making those same costly mistakes.

How Many Battery Watt-Hours (Wh) Do You Actually Need?

Do not trust manufacturer range claims like "Up to 80 miles!". Those numbers are tested with a 60kg rider on a flat velodrome in zero wind on the lowest pedal assist.

As a courier carrying a 10kg food bag on mixed UK roads, you will consume around 13 to 15 Watt-hours per mile:

  • Part-time shift (3–4 hrs / ~25 miles): You need at least 350Wh – 450Wh.
  • Standard dinner shift (5–6 hrs / ~45 miles): You need at least 650Wh – 750Wh (like the NCM Moscow).
  • Full-day weekend grind (8–10 hrs / ~70+ miles): You need 1,000Wh+ (like the Kommoda Pro) or a spare swappable battery.

250W Road Legal vs 1,000W Illegal Motors: The Platform Risk

Under UK EAPC regulations, legal e-bikes must have a 250W continuous rated motor, assist up to 15.5 mph, and require pedalling (no pure throttles).

Many delivery couriers ride illegal 1,000W throttle bikes. In 2026, this is a dangerous gamble:

  1. Police Seizures: City police across London, Manchester, and Sheffield now run targeted operations on delivery riders. Uninsured illegal bikes are seized and crushed.
  2. Platform GPS Speed Bans: Deliveroo and Uber Eats monitor your GPS telemetry. If your average speed is consistently 25–30mph, the system flags vehicle fraud and permanently deletes your courier account with no appeal.

Stick to a legal 250W motor with strong torque (50–70Nm) to conquer hills safely without risking your livelihood.

Backpack vs Rear Rack: Which is Best for Food?

Carrying a Deliveroo cube on your back for 6 hours puts severe strain on your lower spine. Whenever possible, choose a bike with an integrated rear luggage rack rated for at least 25kg, and strap your thermal delivery box directly to the frame. Line the base of the box with high-density foam to absorb road vibration and stop drink cups from leaking.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are you allowed to use an electric bicycle for delivering food in the UK?
Yes. It is 100% legal to deliver food on a road-legal electric bicycle (EAPC) without requiring road tax, MOT, or vehicle insurance. You can register on Deliveroo, Uber Eats, and Just Eat as a bicycle courier immediately.
Can you use an electric scooter to deliver food?
No. Privately owned electric scooters remain illegal on UK public roads and pavements. If delivery platforms discover you are using an e-scooter, they will permanently deactivate your account, and police will confiscate the scooter.
How many miles do you cycle on an average delivery shift?
A typical 4-hour evening dinner shift covers between 25 and 35 miles. Couriers working full-time double shifts regularly cover 50 to 75 miles daily.
